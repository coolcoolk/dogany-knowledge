refraction_notes:
  - note: >
      SHORT IS THE SAFE DEFAULT AFTER A SHORT NIGHT. After a 5-hour night,
      a 10-minute afternoon nap improved sleepiness, alertness and reaction
      lapses straight away and showed no grogginess; 20 minutes also helped;
      30 minutes first made people worse and paid back only from about
      35-95 minutes after waking. A night-shift trial found the same split
      (10 min fine; 30 min still worse 47 minutes later). The minutes are
      minutes ASLEEP: in the 2006 trial the nap was timed from sleep onset,
      so an alarm a few minutes past the target allows for falling asleep.
    grade: C
  - note: >
      GROGGINESS IS THE COST OF A LONGER NAP. Sleep inertia is worst in the
      first 15-30 minutes after waking, is larger after a short night and
      after waking from deep sleep, and longer naps reach deep sleep. That
      is why 009's longer nap (30 to under 60 min) needs its hour of buffer
      before anything that matters, and why the short nap needs only a
      short one. The 2017 review warns that "30 minutes or less avoids
      inertia" is not reliable on its own: prior sleep loss and time of day
      change it.
    grade: B
  - note: >
      THE 90-MINUTE "FULL CYCLE" NAP IS NOT THE BEST BEFORE TRAINING. In 14
      judokas after a short night, a 20-minute post-lunch nap raised
      repeated-sprint power and choice reaction time; a 90-minute nap did
      worse than the 20-minute one and left them sleepier, which the authors
      put down to grogginess. The popular idea that a whole 90-minute cycle
      avoids grogginess is not what this test showed. A long nap is a
      reasonable way to repay sleep on a free day, not a pre-session tool.
    grade: C
  - note: >
      BEFORE AN EVENING SESSION: MOOD YES, STRENGTH NO. After two 4-hour
      nights, 15 resistance-trained men napped 0, 30 or 60 minutes at 13:00
      and lifted at 17:00. Grip, bench press and back squat did not change
      with the nap; alertness, vigour and the word-colour test did. A
      20-minute nap in athletes on a normal schedule also left short
      physical tests unchanged. This matches 009's force null: the nap
      before an evening lift is for how the session feels and for focus,
      not for the numbers on the bar.
    grade: C
  - note: >
      NAP VERSUS CAFFEINE, AND BOTH. In an afternoon driving trial, 150 mg
      of caffeine taken just before a nap of up to 15 minutes cut incidents
      to 9 percent of placebo, against 34 percent for caffeine alone; in a
      second trial caffeine then a 20-minute nap beat the nap alone, light
      or face washing. After a short night in judokas, a 20-minute nap
      helped repeated sprints, caffeine alone did not, and caffeine before
      the nap did best. For memory, a long nap beat caffeine, and caffeine
      impaired motor learning. The combination works because caffeine takes
      effect about as the short nap ends. It does not escape 070: the
      caffeine in a "coffee nap" counts against bedtime like any other dose,
      which after a short night makes the nap alone the first choice.
    grade: C
  - note: >
      DOES THE NAP COST TONIGHT'S SLEEP? A nap in the early evening
      lowered sleep pressure: the following night started later and built
      less deep sleep. A 20-minute post-lunch nap left the following
      night's sleep structure unchanged in athletes. In a 62-person
      actigraphy study, late and frequent naps went with poorer night
      sleep; nap length did not. Direction: early and short is cheap, late
      is what costs. No trial gives a clock time after which a nap harms
      the night; any cutoff the brief speaks is the plan's.
    grade: C
  - note: >
      RESTING WITHOUT SLEEPING STILL COUNTS. In the driving trial, naps that
      were only light dozing were still effective. A user who lies down for
      15-20 minutes and does not fall fully asleep has not wasted the time.
    grade: C
claim: >
  After a short night, the best-supported nap for the same day is a short
  one: about 10-20 minutes asleep in the early afternoon. In a 24-person
  crossover after 5 hours in bed, a 10-minute nap improved sleepiness,
  alertness and reaction lapses immediately with no measurable grogginess;
  20 minutes also helped; 30 minutes produced grogginess first and benefits
  only from about 35-95 minutes after waking, and a night-shift trial found
  the same 10-versus-30 split. Sleep inertia is worst in the first 15-30
  minutes after waking and larger after sleep loss and deep sleep, so a
  longer nap (009's 30 to under 60 minutes) needs about an hour before
  training or driving, and a 90-minute nap before an afternoon test did
  worse than a 20-minute one on repeated sprints. Before an evening lift a
  30-60 minute early-afternoon nap lifted mood and alertness but not grip,
  bench or squat strength (15 trained men after two 4-hour nights). Caffeine
  taken just before a 15-20 minute nap beat either alone on afternoon
  sleepiness, simulated driving and repeated sprints, but its caffeine still
  counts against bedtime (070), so after a short night the nap alone comes
  first. An early-evening nap delays sleep and reduces deep-sleep pressure
  that night, and late or frequent naps go with poorer night sleep; no trial
  sets a clock cutoff. Every trial is small and mostly in young men.

  "which nap, when, after a short night, around training". Brooks & Lack
  2006 is the direct duration trial after restricted sleep and remains the

# sleep-recovery/nap-length-timing-short-night-084 -- 짧게 잔 날의 낮잠: 길이, 시각, 카페인, 저녁 운동

**One line:** after a short night, nap 10-20 minutes in the early afternoon;
longer naps bring grogginess and need an hour before training; a nap before
an evening lift helps how it feels, not how much you lift; caffeine with a
short nap works but still counts against bedtime; late naps are what cost
the night.

| 90 min nap before a test | worse than 20 min on repeated sprints, sleepier | one small trial |
| Nap before a 17:00 lift after two 4 h nights | mood and focus up, strength unchanged | one small trial |

| Last safe nap time | no trial | product only |

## 한국어 요약 (답변용)

- 짧게 잔 날의 낮잠은 이른 오후에 10~20분이 기본이다. 5시간 잔 다음 날 10분 낮잠은 깨자마자 졸림과
  반응 실수가 줄었고 멍한 느낌이 없었다. 20분도 도움이 됐다. 30분은 깬 직후 오히려 더 나빴고, 35~95분이
  지나서야 효과가 났다. 야간 근무 실험에서도 10분과 30분의 차이가 같았다(24명, 31명).
- 분은 '잠든 시간'이다. 잠드는 데 몇 분 걸리니 알람은 목표보다 조금 넉넉하게 맞춘다.
- 깬 직후 멍한 느낌(수면 관성)은 처음 15~30분이 가장 심하다. 짧게 잔 날, 깊이 잠들었다 깼을 때 더
  심하다. 그래서 30~60분 낮잠은 운동이나 운전까지 1시간쯤 여유를 둔다.
- 90분 '한 사이클' 낮잠이 운동 전에 가장 좋다는 근거는 없다. 유도 선수 14명에게서 90분은 20분보다
  반복 스프린트가 나빴고 더 졸렸다. 긴 낮잠은 쉬는 날 잠을 메울 때 쓴다.
- 저녁 웨이트 전 낮잠은 기분과 집중을 올리지만 힘은 올리지 않는다. 4시간씩 이틀 잔 남성 15명이 13시에
  30분이나 60분 자고 17시에 들었을 때 악력, 벤치, 스쿼트는 그대로였고 기분과 각성이 좋아졌다.
- 커피를 마시고 바로 15~20분 자는 '커피 낮잠'은 둘 중 하나만 한 것보다 나았다(운전 실험, 졸림,
  스프린트). 하지만 그 카페인도 밤잠에 영향을 준다(070). 짧게 잔 날은 낮잠만 먼저 권한다.
- 이른 저녁에 낮잠을 자면 그날 밤 잠들기가 늦어지고 깊은 잠이 줄었다. 늦게, 자주 자는 낮잠이 밤잠을
  나쁘게 하는 쪽과 함께 나타났다. 낮잠 길이는 관련이 없었다. 몇 시까지 자도 되는지 정한 연구는 없다.
- 잠들지 못하고 꾸벅꾸벅 졸기만 해도 효과가 있었다. 누워 있었던 시간이 헛되지 않다.
- 모든 연구가 작고 대부분 젊은 남성이다.
