# sleep-recovery/late-night-next-morning-training-080

A late night out is rarely a reason to skip the next morning's training. It
is a reason to change what the session asks for.

Start with the sleep itself. Going to bed late and getting up at the usual
time is the kind of short night that, across the controlled studies, did not
consistently hurt performance. An alarm that cuts the night at the other end
did. And morning sessions are the ones least affected, because the cost of
lost sleep grows with hours awake. So after a late dinner with no or light
drinking, the session can go ahead as planned, minus any attempt at a new
best.

Drinking changes the picture, but not the way most people expect. In the
studies that tested people the morning after about one gram of alcohol per
kilogram (for a 70 kg person, roughly a bottle and a half of soju), maximal
strength did not drop. Mid-thigh pulls, curls, maximal leg contractions,
lower-body strength: all the same as on the sober morning. What dropped was
the fast and the hard: jump height went down, and an all-out cycling bout to
exhaustion was about a tenth shorter. People also felt worse doing the same
work. Hikers who were hungover walked a long gorge in the same time as
everyone else and reported being more exhausted. Rugby players who drank
heavily and slept one to three hours were back to normal two days later.

The clearest cost is in the head. With alcohol nearly gone from the blood,
hungover people still had slower psychomotor speed and worse sustained
attention, and drove worse. That matters more for a snatch, a climbing
route or a sparring round than for a leg press.

The less obvious risk is that the alcohol may not be gone. The body clears
it at a roughly fixed rate, about 15 mg per 100 mL of blood per hour. A heavy
evening that ends after midnight can still be in the blood at a six or seven
o'clock start. That is first a reason not to drive to the gym, and second a
reason to stay away from heavy overhead work, Olympic lifts and anything
where balance or a fall is the risk. It is the one morning where offering
rest is a reasonable first suggestion.

Sleeping somewhere unfamiliar costs one night. In sleep-lab studies the first
night in a new bed is shorter and more broken, with less dream sleep, and it
settles by the second night. People in their twenties showed the effect
least.

One night is not two. Strength studies found that a single night without
sleep did little to strength, while consecutive short nights cut the big
compound lifts from the second night on. A Friday and a Saturday out before
a heavy Sunday squat is a reason to lighten the top sets.

What this does not support is an injury number. The figures that circulate
-- a hangover cutting performance by 11.4 percent, drinkers injured at more
than twice the rate -- trace to a narrative review whose underlying studies
could not be found, and the adult evidence on sleep and injury does not show
a link. The honest caution is about technique and falls, not a multiplier.

  않았다. 줄어든 건 점프 높이와 전력 사이클 지구력(약 11% 짧아짐)이고, 같은 운동이 더 힘들게
  느껴진다. 근력 운동은 하고, 점프·스프린트·인터벌·기록 측정은 다른 날로 바꾸거나 가볍게 한다.

  필요한 운동이 머신·바벨 근력 운동보다 더 위험하다.

  이날만은 쉬자고 먼저 제안해도 된다. 혈중 농도를 계산해 말하지는 않는다.
