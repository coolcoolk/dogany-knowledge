refraction_notes:
  - note: >
      CUE THE EFFECT, NOT THE MUSCLE. Across 73 motor-skill studies, telling
      people where to send an object or how the movement should look
      outside the body ("push the floor away", "reach the hand to the
      ceiling") worked better than telling them which muscle or joint to
      use, a small effect for the same day and a moderate one for what is
      kept. None of those studies used these drills, so it is a phrasing
      preference for the cue, not a promise.
    grade: B
  - note: >
      A NUMBER WITHOUT A UNIT IS NOT A DOSE. The exercise-reporting
      consensus asks for each exercise to be described well enough to repeat
      it and for repetitions and sets to be stated; training apps disagree on
      whether "10 reps" of a one-sided move means 10 in total, 10 a side or
      10 on one side. A plan line has to say which.
    grade: D
  - note: >
      LEG SWINGS AND THE LIKE DO RAISE RANGE FOR A WHILE. A few minutes of
      active dynamic stretching raised hamstring range by 7-10% and it was
      still up 90 minutes later in 24 healthy adults. How many swings are
      needed was not tested, so the count in the plan is a product number.
    grade: C
  - note: >
      THE PULLOVER IS A CHEST-AND-LAT DRILL, MOSTLY CHEST. In eight men the
      barbell pullover worked the pectoralis major harder than the
      latissimus dorsi. As a prep drill it opens the overhead position; it
      is not the lat isolation it is often sold as.
    grade: C
  - note: >
      SIT BACK FOR THORACIC ROTATION. With the hips sat back toward the heels
      the lower back is locked, so the turn has to come from the upper back;
      this is the position clinicians use to measure thoracic rotation, and
      the measurement repeats within about 1-2 degrees.
    grade: C
  - note: >
      CAT-COW IS MOVEMENT, NOT A STRETCH. The practitioner source behind it
      uses a handful of slow cycles (5-8) through a comfortable range to
      loosen the back, not end-range holds.
    grade: D
claim: >
  Drill cues should name the movement's effect rather than a muscle: an
  external focus improved performance (g 0.26) and learning (g 0.58) over an
  internal one across motor-skill studies, though none used these drills. A
  rep count must name its unit, because reporting consensus asks for it and
  common conventions conflict on one-sided moves. Of the eleven prep drills,
  only three have drill-relevant measurement: active dynamic stretching raises
  hamstring range for up to 90 min (leg swing, indirect), the lumbar-locked
  kneeling position confines rotation to the thorax (thoracic rotation), and
  the pullover works the chest more than the lats. The cat-cow's 5-8 slow
  cycles, the bird dog's short holds and the roll-down's vertebra-by-vertebra
  cue are practitioner sources; the bear crawl has one case EMG; arm circles,
  the world's greatest stretch and the halo have nothing beyond coaching
  description. Step order, the common fault and the rep definition for every
  drill are therefore curated craft.
reasoning: >
  This row exists so 741's step lists and rep units carry an honest grade.
  Whether a warm-up helps at all is settled elsewhere (022: the whole warm-up
  carries the effect; 065: no shoulder drill is evidenced over another), so
  the drill cue question is execution quality and clarity, not performance.
  Chua 2021 is the strongest evidence on cue wording; it is a broad
  motor-learning meta-analysis, indirect for warm-up drills, kept at B for the
  direction only. CERT is a consensus on reporting, not an outcome, so the
  rep-unit rule is D; it is in this row because it is the only formal
  statement that exercise dose needs units. Iwata 2019 tested active knee
  extension, not swings, at one dose. Johnson 2012 is a measurement study,
  not a training study. Marchetti 2011 is EMG in eight men (013: activation
  is not outcome), and an older study disagrees. The bear walk data is one
  person. McGill's cat-camel count is an expert essay. All sources were read
  only through search-index renderings, so the re-read is logged in GAPS.

# exercise/drill-cue-rep-unit-evidence-740 -- 준비 운동 동작 큐와 "1회" 정의: 근거가 있는 것과 없는 것

**한 줄 그림:** 큐는 근육 이름보다 동작의 결과("바닥을 밀어낸다")로 말하는 게 낫다. 횟수에는 단위(한쪽,
방향별, 왕복)를 반드시 붙인다. 열한 가지 준비 동작 중 동작 자체를 잰 연구는 세 개뿐이고, 나머지는 코치·임상
현장의 설명이다.

## 한국어 요약 (답변용)

- 큐는 "광배근에 힘을 줘라"처럼 근육을 짚는 말보다 "손끝을 천장으로 뻗는다", "바닥을 밀어낸다"처럼 동작의
  결과를 말하는 쪽이 운동 수행과 학습에 조금 더 낫다. 73개 연구를 묶은 결과다. 다만 이 준비 동작들로 실험한
  연구는 없어서, 표현을 고르는 기준일 뿐 효과를 약속하지는 않는다.
- 횟수에는 단위를 붙인다. "10회"가 양쪽 합쳐 10회인지, 한쪽 10회인지 앱마다 다르게 센다. 운동 보고 지침도
  동작과 횟수를 다시 따라 할 수 있을 만큼 적으라고 한다.
- 레그 스윙 같은 동적 스트레칭은 잠깐 가동 범위를 늘린다. 햄스트링 동적 스트레칭 몇 분 뒤 가동 범위가
  7-10% 늘었고 90분 뒤에도 유지됐다. 몇 번을 해야 하는지는 연구가 없다.
- 무릎 꿇고 엉덩이를 발뒤꿈치 쪽으로 앉힌 자세에서 하는 흉추 회전은 허리가 잠겨서 등 윗부분만 돌게 된다.
  임상에서 흉추 회전을 잴 때 쓰는 자세다.
- 풀오버는 광배근보다 가슴 근육이 더 많이 쓰였다(8명 연구). 준비 운동으로는 팔을 머리 위로 올리는 자세를
  여는 동작이지, 광배근 고립 운동이 아니다.
- 캣카우는 늘리는 스트레칭이 아니라 움직임이다. 편한 범위에서 천천히 5-8번 왕복하는 것이 출처의 방식이다.
- 동작 순서, 흔한 실수, "1회"의 정의는 열한 동작 모두 연구가 아니라 현장 정리다.
