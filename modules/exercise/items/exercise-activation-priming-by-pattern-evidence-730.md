# Activation sprint 2026-10-07.
# The question: before a heavy free-weight compound (bench / horizontal press,
# overhead press, squat, hinge, row), does a short "activation" or
# stabilizer-priming drill -- serratus punch or plate pullover, light fly,
# glute bridge or band walk, bracing drill, band pull-apart -- add anything
# to the specific ramp, at what dose, and when does it cost performance?
# 065 already answers this for the SHOULDER before pressing (ramp is the
# prep; cuff / scapular primer only at a caution rung; no drill tested) and
# 078 for the KNEE before squatting (hip work as a multi-week programme;
# in-session placement untested). This row covers the other patterns, the
# lower-body glute-activation trials, and the pre-fatigue (pre-exhaustion)
# trials that mark where priming turns into a cost. The engine half is 731
# (activation_rules).

priming_evidence:
  - pattern: all heavy compounds
    finding: the specific ramp of the lift itself carries the warm-up effect; adding a general component in front is not consistently better
    basis: exercise/shoulder-prep-specific-vs-general-065; exercise/warmup-ramp-sets-heavy-compounds-080
    basis_grade: B
  - pattern: squat / hinge (lower body)
    finding: a low-load glute series raised jump power against no warm-up; against a control warm-up it lowered glute EMG with no force change before an 80% hang pull, lowered jump height for up to 6 min, gave a group-only sprint gain, and lowered glute and quad EMG in a loaded squat
    basis: Crow 2012; Parr 2017; Comyns 2015; Harrison and McCabe 2017; IJES abstract
    basis_grade: C
  - pattern: squat (knee)
    finding: a band at the knees raises glute EMG but does not correct knee valgus; hip work for a painful knee works as a multi-week programme, placement before squats untested
    basis: Forman 2023; exercise/knee-prep-hip-adductor-abductor-078
    basis_grade: C
  - pattern: horizontal press
    finding: a hard fly (pec deck at 10RM) before the bench lowered bench performance and shifted work to the triceps without raising pec EMG; a hard triceps set before the bench lowered total volume load
    basis: Gentil 2007; Soares 2016
    basis_grade: C
  - pattern: horizontal / vertical press (shoulder)
    finding: no trial of a serratus punch, plate pullover, light fly, external rotation or face pull before pressing; fatiguing the shoulder muscles changes scapular and humeral motion in arm elevation
    basis: exercise/shoulder-prep-specific-vs-general-065; Ebaugh 2006
    basis_grade: D
  - pattern: squat / hinge (trunk)
    finding: no trial of trunk pre-fatigue or a bracing primer before a heavy squat or deadlift; convention puts fatiguing trunk work after the main lifts
    basis: exercise/core-functional-accessory-carry-081
    basis_grade: D
  - pattern: row
    finding: no trial of a scapular primer (pull-apart, prone Y, retraction drill) before rowing
    basis: none located
    basis_grade: D
  - pattern: any (fatigue)
    finding: leg extension to failure before the leg press lowered quad EMG in the leg press; pre-fatigue moves work to synergists rather than raising the target muscle's share
    basis: Augustsson 2003 (via citing papers); Gentil 2007
    basis_grade: C
  - pattern: any (potentiation)
    finding: "activation" from light drills is not potentiation; twitch potentiation fades in under a minute and the voluntary gain after a conditioning set needs an intense one, not a light drill
    basis: Blazevich and Babault 2019
    basis_grade: C
refraction_notes:
  - note: >
      THE RAMP IS THE PRIMER. For every heavy compound, the warm-up with
      evidence is the lift itself, lighter, climbing toward the working
      weight. A general or "activation" piece in front of it has not been
      shown to add to it. A primer is an optional extra, never a
      replacement for the ramp sets.
    grade: B
  - note: >
      GLUTE ACTIVATION BEFORE SQUATS AND DEADLIFTS: MIXED AT BEST. A short
      series of light glute exercises raised jump power compared with doing
      no warm-up at all. Compared with a normal warm-up it did not raise
      force in a heavy pull, glute muscle activity actually dropped a
      little, jump height fell for the first few minutes, and in a loaded
      squat glute and thigh activity fell after it. If someone likes a few
      bridges or band walks, they are harmless when kept light and done
      early, but they are not a performance tool.
    grade: C
  - note: >
      HARD "PRIMING" OF A WORKING MUSCLE COSTS THE BIG LIFT. A hard fly or
      triceps set before the bench, or leg extensions to failure before a
      leg press, lowered the compound's reps or volume and pushed the work
      onto other muscles; it did not make the target muscle work more. A
      primer before a heavy lift stays light and far from failure; the
      same drills done hard belong after the compound.
    grade: C
  - note: >
      NO SERRATUS, PULLOVER, FLY, BRACING OR SCAPULAR PRIMER HAS BEEN
      TESTED. Nothing located measures a heavy bench, overhead press, squat,
      deadlift or row after a serratus punch, plate pullover, light fly,
      bracing drill or band pull-apart. "It switches the muscle on" is an
      EMG statement, and EMG is not an outcome. These drills are a cue or a
      preference, chosen by the lifter, never presented as protective or as
      adding strength.
    grade: D
  - note: >
      KEEP THE TRUNK AND SHOULDER STABILISERS FRESH. Tiring the shoulder
      muscles changed how the shoulder blade and upper arm moved in
      lifting the arm, and convention keeps fatiguing core work after the
      main lifts. Neither has been tested as a cost to a heavy lift; the
      rule is a precaution, not a measured effect.
    grade: D
claim: >
  Before a heavy free-weight compound, the warm-up that carries the evidence is
  the lift itself done lighter: a few ramp sets climbing toward the working
  load. Pattern-specific "activation" drills have little or no evidence of
  adding to it. For the lower body, a low-load gluteal series raised jump
  power only against no warm-up at all; against a control warm-up it lowered
  glute activity slightly with no force change before an 80% hang pull,
  lowered jump height for up to six minutes, gave a group-only sprint gain,
  and in one loaded-squat abstract lowered glute and thigh activity. For the
  press, row and trunk no trial of any primer -- serratus punch, plate
  pullover, light fly, bracing drill, pull-apart -- before a heavy lift was
  located, for performance or for injury; 065 already reaches the same
  verdict for shoulder cuff drills before pressing. Where priming turns into
  a cost is clear in direction: hard sets of a prime mover or synergist just
  before the compound (pec deck or triceps before the bench, leg extension
  before the leg press) lower the compound's reps or volume and shift work to
  other muscles. So a primer, if used, is one or two drills for one light,
  far-from-failure set each, placed before the ramp, and never a substitute
  for the ramp sets; light drill "activation" is not potentiation.
reasoning: >
  The question splits into three evidence layers. The ramp layer is strong
  and already graded in 065 and 080, so it is borrowed, not re-argued. The
  glute-activation layer has the most direct trials, but they are small,
  test jumps, sprints or a hang pull rather than a heavy squat or deadlift,
  and disagree: Crow 2012 is positive only against a no-warm-up control,
  which says nothing about adding a primer to a ramp; Parr 2017 with a
  proper control is null to slightly negative; Comyns 2015 shows a transient
  drop; Harrison and McCabe find group but not individual gains. That is a
  C for "mixed, no consistent benefit", not evidence of harm. The pre-fatigue
  layer is consistent in direction across Gentil 2007, Soares 2016 and
  Augustsson 2003 (cited), all at 10RM to failure, which is far harder than a
  primer; it grounds the fatigue boundary, not a specific safe dose. Ebaugh
  2006 is mechanism only. For serratus, pullover, fly, bracing and scapular
  primers nothing was located, so they stay D and drill choice is
  preference. Blazevich and Babault 2019 is why "activation" is not sold as
  potentiation. The row is contested because practitioner content
  (019) recommends activation as a cue while the trials do not support a
  performance gain. All new abstracts were read only as search-index
  renderings, so numbers are carried sparingly and the re-read is logged in
  GAPS.

# exercise/activation-priming-by-pattern-evidence-730 -- 무거운 복합 운동 전 "활성화" 드릴: 동작별 근거와 피로의 경계

**한 줄 그림:** 무거운 스쿼트·데드리프트·벤치·오버헤드 프레스·로우 전에 근거가 있는 준비는 그 운동을
가볍게 올려 가는 램프 세트다. 전거근 펀치, 플레이트 풀오버, 가벼운 플라이, 글루트 브릿지 같은 "활성화"
드릴은 거기에 더해 준다는 근거가 없다. 하더라도 가볍게, 램프 앞에, 1-2개만 한다. 같은 근육을 세게
미리 지치게 하면 본운동이 떨어진다.

## Priming evidence by pattern (engine-readable)

| Pattern | What was tested | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| all heavy compounds | specific ramp vs general / none | the ramp carries the effect | review of RCTs (065, 080) |
| squat / hinge | low-load glute series | beats no warm-up for jump power; vs control warm-up: no force gain, glute EMG down, jump down for 6 min, squat EMG down | 4 small acute trials + 1 abstract |
| squat (knee) | band at the knees; hip work for a sore knee | glute EMG up, valgus not fixed; hip work works over weeks | review (078) |
| horizontal press | pec deck or triceps to failure first | bench reps / volume down, work shifted to triceps | 2 small crossovers |
| press (shoulder) | serratus, pullover, fly, ER, face pull first | not tested | none |
| squat / hinge (trunk) | core fatigue or bracing primer first | not tested; fatiguing core after | none (081) |
| row | scapular primer first | not tested | none |
| any | light drill as "potentiation" | not potentiation; needs an intense set | review |

## 한국어 요약 (답변용)

- 무거운 복합 운동 전에 효과가 확인된 준비는 그 운동 자체를 가볍게 몇 세트 올려 가는 것이다. 일반
  워밍업이나 활성화 드릴을 앞에 붙인다고 더 좋아진다는 근거는 없다. 드릴은 선택 사항이고, 램프 세트를
  대신하지 못한다.
- 엉덩이(글루트) 활성화는 결과가 엇갈린다. 가벼운 글루트 운동 묶음은 아무 워밍업도 안 했을 때보다는
  점프 파워를 올렸다. 하지만 보통 워밍업과 비교하면 80% 무게의 하이 행 풀에서 힘이 늘지 않았고 엉덩이
  근육 활동은 오히려 조금 줄었다. 직후 몇 분 동안 점프 높이가 떨어진 연구도 있고, 바벨 스쿼트에서
  엉덩이·허벅지 근육 활동이 줄었다는 학회 초록도 있다. 브릿지나 밴드 워크가 좋으면 가볍게, 일찍 하면
  해는 없지만 기록을 올리는 도구는 아니다.
- 무릎에 밴드를 걸고 스쿼트하면 엉덩이 근전도는 올라가지만 무릎이 안으로 모이는 건 고쳐지지 않았다.
  무릎이 아픈 사람의 엉덩이 운동은 몇 주 프로그램으로 효과가 있고, 스쿼트 직전에 하는 효과는 연구되지
  않았다(078).
- 같은 근육을 세게 미리 지치게 하면 본운동이 떨어진다. 펙덱이나 삼두 운동을 10RM으로 먼저 하면 벤치
  횟수·볼륨이 줄고 일이 삼두로 넘어갔다. 레그 익스텐션을 실패까지 하고 레그프레스를 하면 대퇴사두
  근전도가 줄었다. 준비 드릴은 가볍게, 실패와 멀게 하고, 세게 할 거면 본운동 뒤로 보낸다.
- 전거근 펀치, 플레이트 풀오버, 가벼운 플라이, 브레이싱 드릴, 밴드 풀어파트를 한 뒤 무거운 벤치·
  오버헤드 프레스·스쿼트·데드리프트·로우가 좋아지는지 본 연구는 찾지 못했다. "근육이 깨어난다"는 말은
  근전도 이야기이고 효과의 증거가 아니다. 감각을 잡는 큐나 취향으로 쓰고, 부상을 막는다고 말하지 않는다.
- 어깨 근육을 지치게 하면 팔을 들 때 견갑골과 위팔의 움직임이 바뀌었다. 코어를 지치게 하는 운동은
  본운동 뒤에 두는 게 관례다. 둘 다 무거운 리프트에 손해라는 게 측정된 건 아니고, 조심하는 규칙이다.
- 가벼운 드릴의 "활성화"는 활성 후 강화(PAP/PAPE)가 아니다. 그건 강한 수축 뒤에 생기는 효과다.
- 새로 인용한 연구는 모두 검색 색인에 보인 초록으로만 읽었다. 원문 재확인 목록은 GAPS에 있다.
