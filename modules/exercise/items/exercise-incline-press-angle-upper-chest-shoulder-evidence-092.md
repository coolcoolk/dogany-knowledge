refraction_notes:
  - note: >
      INCLINE BIASES THE UPPER CHEST, ON THIN DATA. In the one training
      trial, untrained men who only pressed on a 44-degree incline grew the
      upper chest site more than those who only pressed flat or split their
      sets between both; the middle and lower sites did not differ. Ten men
      per group, eight weeks, once a week, on a Smith machine. Speak it as
      "incline seems to bias the upper chest", never as proven or as a size
      of effect, and say it has not been tested in trained lifters.
    grade: C
  - note: >
      FLAT IS NOT USELESS FOR THE UPPER CHEST, AND INCLINE IS NOT NEEDED TO
      GROW IT. The flat-only group's upper site also grew in absolute terms,
      and the middle and lower sites grew alike in all groups. Several EMG
      studies find the upper fibres work substantially on a flat bench too,
      and one older study found no incline advantage at all.
    grade: C
  - note: >
      THE PEAK ANGLE IS NOT SETTLED. Upper-fibre EMG peaked at 30 degrees in
      one study (with 15 not different from 30) and at about 43-44 degrees
      in two others; a 20-degree bench was lower than 32 or 43 in one study.
      What is consistent: the steeper the bench, the more the front deltoid
      works and the less the middle and lower chest work; past about 45
      degrees the lift behaves more like a shoulder press. A low-to-moderate
      incline (roughly 15-30 degrees, at most 45) is a reasonable range, not
      an optimum.
    grade: C
  - note: >
      "INCLINE IS EASIER ON THE SHOULDER" IS NOT ESTABLISHED. No study
      located compares shoulder pain, injury or joint load between incline
      and flat pressing. Mechanism cuts both ways: a steeper bench moves the
      upper arm from horizontal pushing toward overhead flexion and loads
      the front deltoid more, while an MRI model found the least modelled
      rotator-cuff compression when the arm is raised forward at lower
      angles. Practitioner sources list incline as one thing to try when flat
      pressing hurts, not as the shoulder-safe choice; some people tolerate
      one and not the other. Grip width and scapular position have more
      (still model-level) support than bench angle.
    grade: D
claim: >
  Bench angle shifts which part of the chest does the work. In the only
  training trial located, untrained men pressing on a 44-degree incline grew
  the upper (clavicular) chest site more than men pressing flat or splitting
  sets between both, with no difference at middle and lower sites (10 men per
  group, 8 weeks, Smith machine), and an acute ultrasound study found
  post-exercise swelling followed the same split. EMG studies agree that a
  steeper bench lowers middle and lower chest activity and raises front
  deltoid activity, but disagree on where upper-chest activity peaks (30
  degrees in one study, about 43-44 in two, no incline advantage in an older
  one), and EMG does not predict growth. A low-to-moderate incline of about
  15-30 degrees, not past 45, is a reasonable range for an upper-chest press.
  Whether incline is easier or harder on the shoulder has not been studied:
  no trial or cohort compares shoulder pain, injury or joint load by bench
  angle; mechanism points both ways and practitioners treat incline as one
  option to try, not as the shoulder-safe press.
reasoning: >
  Chaves 2020 is a properly randomised parallel trial with an ANCOVA on
  baseline and a confidence interval that clears zero, but its thickness
  sample is 10 per arm, untrained, once weekly and on a Smith machine, so it
  supports a direction (C), not a magnitude; the large relative changes in
  every group are typical of untrained ultrasound data and are not carried
  as effect sizes. The 2022 acute study is consistent but measures swelling,
  which 013 rules out as a growth proxy. The angle EMG set (Barnett 1995,
  Trebs 2010, Lauver 2016, Rodriguez-Ridao 2020, Chernov 2025) is small and
  internally inconsistent on the upper-fibre peak, and consistent only on
  the deltoid-up / sternal-down trend; a 2025 three-angle EMG study in
  beginners (J Phys Ther Sci) was retracted and is not used. The shoulder
  half has no outcome study at all: Lawrence 2018 models tendon proximity
  under imposed elevation without load, Noteboom 2024 modelled only flat
  pressing, and the practitioner source does not rank incline. That half is
  held at D and the item is contested. injury_history is hard with
  block_specifics, matching 064: shoulder-site advice is withheld until the
  user's rung is known.

# exercise/incline-press-angle-upper-chest-shoulder-evidence-092 -- 인클라인 각도와 윗가슴, 그리고 어깨

**한 줄 그림:** 인클라인은 윗가슴 쪽으로 성장을 조금 더 몰아 줄 가능성이 있다(작은 연구 하나).
각도는 대략 15-30도, 45도를 넘기지 않는 정도면 된다. "인클라인이 어깨에 더 편하다"는
연구로 확인된 말이 아니다.

## What each angle does (evidence map)

| Question | Finding | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Does incline grow the upper chest more? | 44-degree incline only > flat only or split, upper site only (Chaves 2020) | one small RCT, untrained, Smith |
| Does flat grow the upper chest? | yes, in absolute terms; middle / lower sites alike in all groups | same trial |
| Best angle for upper-fibre EMG | 30 (Rodriguez-Ridao 2020); ~43-44 (Trebs 2010, Chernov 2025); no incline edge (Barnett 1995) | EMG, small, inconsistent |
| Front deltoid by angle | rises as the bench rises, above flat from ~30 degrees | EMG, consistent |
| Middle / lower chest by angle | highest flat, falls as the bench rises | EMG, consistent |
| Shoulder pain / injury by angle | not studied | none |
| Shoulder load by angle | no pressing study; arm moves toward overhead flexion as the bench rises | mechanism / model |

## 한국어 요약 (답변용)

- 인클라인 벤치만 한 사람이 플랫만 한 사람이나 둘을 나눠 한 사람보다 윗가슴 두께가 더 늘었다는
  연구가 하나 있다. 운동 초보 남성, 그룹당 10명, 주 1회 8주, 스미스 머신이었다. 가운데·아래
  가슴은 차이가 없었다. "윗가슴 쪽으로 조금 더 가는 것 같다" 정도로 말하고, 운동 경력자에게서는
  확인되지 않았다고 덧붙인다.
- 플랫 벤치도 윗가슴을 키운다. 윗가슴을 키우려면 인클라인이 꼭 있어야 한다는 말은 근거가 없다.
- 몇 도가 최선인지는 정해지지 않았다. 근전도로 보면 윗가슴은 30도에서 가장 높았다는 연구와
  43-44도에서 가장 높았다는 연구가 엇갈린다. 일관된 건 벤치를 세울수록 앞어깨가 더 일하고
  가운데·아래 가슴은 덜 일한다는 점이다. 45도를 넘으면 숄더 프레스에 가까워진다. 15-30도,
  최대 45도를 쓸 만한 범위로 본다. 근전도는 근성장을 예측하지 못한다는 점도 잊지 않는다.
- "인클라인이 어깨에 더 편한가?"에는 "연구된 적이 없다"가 정답이다. 인클라인과 플랫의 어깨
  통증·부상·관절 부하를 비교한 연구가 없다. 벤치를 세우면 팔이 머리 위로 드는 방향에 가까워지고
  앞어깨 부담이 커지지만, 팔을 앞으로 낮게 드는 자세가 회전근개 압박이 가장 적었다는 모델 연구도
  있어 방향이 한쪽으로 정리되지 않는다. 사람마다 플랫이 괜찮고 인클라인이 아프거나 그 반대다.
  어깨에 대해서는 각도보다 그립 폭(어깨너비 1.5배 이내)과 날개뼈 모으기 쪽 근거가 조금 더 있다
  (그것도 모델 수준).
